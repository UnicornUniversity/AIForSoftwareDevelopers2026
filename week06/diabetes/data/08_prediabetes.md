# Prediabetes

Source: ADA Standards of Care (2024); WHO *Definition and Diagnosis of Diabetes Mellitus and Intermediate Hyperglycaemia* (2006)
Source type: Educational adaptation of clinical guidelines
Topic: Prediabetes — definitions, interpretation, and prevention of type 2 diabetes
Educational adaptation: Concise summary of publicly available guidance; not a clinical decision tool
Last reviewed: 2024-01

## Overview

Prediabetes describes a state of glycaemia above normal but below the diagnostic threshold for diabetes. It identifies people at elevated risk of progressing to type 2 diabetes and of developing cardiovascular disease. The term is used by the American Diabetes Association; the World Health Organization uses "intermediate hyperglycaemia" and does not endorse an HbA1c range for it.

## Key information

Prediabetes is defined by any one of the following (ADA):

- **Impaired fasting glucose (IFG)**: FPG 5.6–6.9 mmol/L (100–125 mg/dL).
- **Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT)**: 2-hour plasma glucose 7.8–11.0 mmol/L (140–199 mg/dL) on a 75 g OGTT.
- **HbA1c 5.7%–6.4% (39–46 mmol/mol)**.

WHO thresholds differ slightly: IFG is defined as FPG 6.1–6.9 mmol/L (110–125 mg/dL); IGT thresholds are the same.

Prediabetes is associated with an annualized rate of progression to type 2 diabetes of approximately 5–10% in adults, higher in those meeting multiple criteria. Not everyone with prediabetes progresses; a substantial fraction revert to normoglycaemia with lifestyle change.

**Prevention.** The Diabetes Prevention Program (DPP) and similar trials demonstrated that intensive lifestyle intervention — targeting ≥ 7% weight loss and ≥ 150 minutes/week of moderate-intensity physical activity — reduces the incidence of type 2 diabetes by approximately 58% over three years in adults with IGT. Metformin reduces incidence by approximately 31% and is often considered for individuals with BMI ≥ 35 kg/m², age < 60 years, prior GDM, or rising HbA1c despite lifestyle effort.

Screening cadence for adults with prediabetes: annual retesting.

## Important distinctions / exceptions

The three prediabetes criteria (IFG, IGT, HbA1c 5.7–6.4%) identify partially overlapping populations. IGT is generally the strongest predictor of progression to type 2 diabetes. HbA1c in the 5.7–6.4% range is the least specific.

Prediabetes is not a benign label. It is a signal to act, not a milder disease. Some clinicians and researchers argue against the term because it may imply inevitability of progression; the alternative view is that the term motivates preventive action.

Prediabetes thresholds are calibrated to future diabetes risk, not to current end-organ damage. Values of, for example, HbA1c 6.4% and 6.5% differ by 0.1 percentage point and are not meaningfully different biologically; the diagnostic line is a threshold, not a step change.

## Examples

A 48-year-old with BMI 30 kg/m² has an HbA1c of 6.1% and an FPG of 6.4 mmol/L. Both are in the prediabetes range. Lifestyle intervention is offered; metformin is considered given the BMI. Annual repeat testing is arranged.

A 65-year-old with HbA1c 5.9% and IFG has a strong preference for lifestyle rather than medication. Structured group education and follow-up in one year are arranged; two years later the HbA1c is 5.6%, reflecting reversion to normoglycaemia.

A 42-year-old has an HbA1c of 6.4% on a routine screen; a confirmatory HbA1c one week later is 6.7%. The patient has moved from the prediabetes range to meeting the criterion for diabetes; the case is reclassified (see **01_diabetes_diagnosis.md**).
