# Roasting

Green coffee has almost no aroma or flavor. **Roasting** is the thermal transformation that unlocks the ~800+ volatile aromatic compounds present in a brewed cup.

## What happens inside the bean

During roasting, the bean is heated from ~20 °C to 190–230 °C over 8–20 minutes. Several chemical and physical changes occur:

- **Drying phase (0–5 min, up to ~160 °C):** water evaporates; bean turns from green to yellow. Bready aroma.
- **Maillard reactions (~150–200 °C):** amino acids and reducing sugars combine, producing hundreds of aromatic compounds. Bean turns brown.
- **Caramelization (~170–200 °C):** sugars break down, producing caramel and toffee notes.
- **First crack (~196–205 °C):** water vapor expands and cracks the bean audibly. This is the earliest point a coffee can be considered "drinkable."
- **Development phase (after first crack):** roaster adjusts time between first crack and drop to shape the final flavor. Typically 15–25% of total roast time.
- **Second crack (~220–225 °C):** cellulose structure breaks down, oils migrate to the surface. Bean is now dark and oily.

## Roast levels

| Roast level | Internal temperature | Color | Common name |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | 196–205 °C | Light brown, dry | Cinnamon, New England, Nordic |
| Medium | 210–220 °C | Medium brown, dry | American, City |
| Medium-dark | 220–225 °C | Rich brown, hint of oil | Full City, Vienna |
| Dark | 225–230+ °C | Dark brown to black, oily | French, Italian, Espresso |

**General rule:** the lighter the roast, the more the coffee tastes like its origin (acidity, fruit, floral notes). The darker the roast, the more it tastes like the roast itself (bitter, smoky, chocolate, char).

## Weight and volume changes

During roasting a bean:

- **Loses ~15–20% of its weight** (mostly water and CO₂).
- **Expands ~50–100% in volume** (like popcorn).
- **Absorbs energy equivalent to ~600 kJ per kg** in a commercial drum roaster.

## Freshness and rest

- Freshly roasted coffee releases CO₂ for several days — a process called **degassing**.
- **Espresso:** best 5–14 days after roast.
- **Filter (V60, Chemex, French press):** best 3–21 days after roast.
- After ~4 weeks in a sealed bag, aromatic compounds noticeably decline.
- Once opened, aim to use within 2–3 weeks; store in a cool, dark, airtight container.

**Never refrigerate whole beans.** Coffee absorbs moisture and odors, and repeated warming/cooling cycles cause condensation inside the bag. Freezing in a sealed, portioned bag is acceptable for long-term storage — but only thaw once, and never re-freeze.
