# Coffee Plant and Varieties

## The coffee plant

Coffee comes from the seed (commonly called the "bean") of a small evergreen tree of the genus **Coffea**. Although there are more than 120 known species, only two are of significant commercial importance: **Coffea arabica** (Arabica) and **Coffea canephora** (Robusta).

Coffee trees prefer:

- **Temperature:** 15–24 °C for Arabica, 24–30 °C for Robusta.
- **Rainfall:** 1,500–2,500 mm per year, well distributed.
- **Altitude:** Arabica thrives at 1,000–2,200 m; Robusta grows well from sea level up to about 800 m.
- **Soil:** Deep, well-drained, slightly acidic (pH 6–6.5), rich in organic matter.

A young coffee tree produces its first commercial harvest 3–4 years after planting and continues to yield for 20–30 years.

## Arabica vs. Robusta

| Attribute | Arabica | Robusta |
|---|---|---|
| Share of world production | ~60% | ~40% |
| Caffeine content | 1.2–1.5% | 2.2–2.7% |
| Flavor profile | Sweet, complex, acidic, floral, fruity | Strong, bitter, earthy, woody, less acidic |
| Body | Lighter | Heavier |
| Sugar content (green bean) | ~6–9% | ~3–7% |
| Chlorogenic acids | ~5.5–8% | ~7–10% |
| Disease resistance | Lower (susceptible to leaf rust) | Higher |
| Typical price (green) | Higher | Lower |
| Best use | Specialty coffee, filter, espresso blends | Instant coffee, espresso blends (for crema and body) |

## Common Arabica cultivars

- **Typica** — the "original" cultivar; balanced, sweet, mild acidity.
- **Bourbon** — a natural mutation of Typica; sweeter, richer body.
- **Caturra** — a dwarf Bourbon mutation; bright acidity, easy to farm.
- **Geisha (Gesha)** — Ethiopian origin; floral, jasmine-and-bergamot aroma; commands very high prices at auction.
- **SL28 / SL34** — Kenyan selections; blackcurrant and citrus notes.
- **Pacamara** — Salvadoran hybrid; large beans, complex chocolate-and-fruit profile.

## Where coffee grows: "The Bean Belt"

Coffee grows in a band around the equator between the Tropic of Cancer and the Tropic of Capricorn — roughly 25° N to 30° S. The largest producers in 2024 were:

1. **Brazil** — ~40% of world production; mostly Arabica, some Robusta ("Conilon").
2. **Vietnam** — ~15%; predominantly Robusta.
3. **Colombia** — ~8%; 100% Arabica by law.
4. **Indonesia** — ~7%; both species.
5. **Ethiopia** — ~5%; the botanical birthplace of Arabica.

Ethiopia is also the only country where coffee still grows **wild** in native forests. Roughly 15 million Ethiopians depend on coffee for their livelihood.
