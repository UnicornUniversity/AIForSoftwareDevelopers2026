# Harvesting and Processing

The coffee "bean" is actually the seed of a fruit called a **coffee cherry**. Turning ripe cherries into green coffee ready for export requires two distinct stages: **harvesting** and **processing**.

## Harvesting

There are two main harvesting methods:

- **Selective (hand) picking** — pickers pass through the same tree multiple times over several weeks, picking only fully ripe cherries. Labor-intensive but produces the highest quality; standard for specialty Arabica in Central America and Ethiopia.
- **Strip picking** — all cherries on a branch are stripped at once, regardless of ripeness. Faster and cheaper; used for commodity coffee and mechanical harvesting on flat terrain (Brazil).

A skilled hand-picker collects 50–100 kg of cherries per day. Roughly **5 kg of cherries yield 1 kg of green coffee**, and about 1 kg of green coffee yields **0.8 kg of roasted coffee**, which brews around **120 cups of espresso**.

## Processing methods

Processing removes the fruit around the seed. The chosen method has a **profound impact on cup flavor** — arguably as much as the origin.

### 1. Washed (wet) process

The traditional method for high-quality Arabica.

Steps:
1. **De-pulping** — a mechanical de-pulper removes the outer skin and most of the fruit within 24 hours of picking.
2. **Fermentation** — pulped beans sit in water tanks for 12–48 hours; microorganisms break down the remaining mucilage.
3. **Washing** — beans are rinsed thoroughly.
4. **Drying** — spread on patios or raised beds for 1–2 weeks until moisture content reaches ~11%.

**Flavor profile:** clean, bright, acidic, transparent to origin. Common in Colombia, Kenya, Ethiopia (Yirgacheffe washed), Guatemala.

### 2. Natural (dry) process

The oldest method — used for millennia in Ethiopia and Yemen.

Steps:
1. Whole cherries are laid out to dry in the sun for 2–6 weeks, turned regularly.
2. Once dried, the shriveled fruit and parchment are removed mechanically.

**Flavor profile:** fruity, sweet, wine-like, heavy body. Common in Ethiopia (Harrar, Sidamo naturals), Brazil, Yemen.

### 3. Honey (pulped natural) process

A hybrid method developed in Central America.

Steps:
1. Cherries are de-pulped like the washed method.
2. Beans are dried **with the sticky mucilage still attached** — no water fermentation.
3. The color of the mucilage during drying gives sub-styles: **white honey** (light), **yellow honey**, **red honey**, **black honey** (longest drying, most mucilage retained, sweetest).

**Flavor profile:** sweetness of natural + clarity of washed. Common in Costa Rica, El Salvador, Honduras.

### 4. Anaerobic / experimental processing

Cherries or beans are sealed in oxygen-free tanks for controlled fermentation (24–120 hours) before drying. Produces bold, unusual flavors — tropical fruit, cinnamon, whiskey, red wine. Popular in specialty coffee since the mid-2010s, especially in Colombia and Panama.

## Grading and sorting

Once dried, green coffee is:

1. **Hulled** to remove the parchment layer.
2. **Sorted by size** using screens (e.g., Screen 17, Screen 18).
3. **Sorted by density** on gravity tables.
4. **Sorted by color** — modern mills use optical sorters to eject defects.
5. **Graded** according to origin-specific systems (e.g., Kenya: AA, AB, PB; Colombia: Supremo, Excelso; Ethiopia: Grade 1–5; specialty: SCA score 80+).

Only coffee scoring **80 or above** on the 100-point Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) scale can be labeled **specialty coffee**. Roughly 10% of global production qualifies.
