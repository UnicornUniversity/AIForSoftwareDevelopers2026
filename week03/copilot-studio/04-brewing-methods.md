# Brewing Methods

Brewing is the extraction of soluble compounds — flavors, oils, caffeine — from ground coffee using hot (or cold) water. The **grind size**, **water temperature**, **brew time**, and **coffee-to-water ratio** determine the outcome.

## The four key variables

1. **Grind size** — finer grind, faster extraction (more surface area). Espresso needs a fine grind; French press needs a coarse grind.
2. **Water temperature** — 90–96 °C is optimal for most methods. Below 85 °C: under-extracted, sour. Above 96 °C: over-extracted, bitter.
3. **Brew time** — from ~25 seconds (espresso) to 24 hours (cold brew).
4. **Ratio** — how many grams of coffee to how many grams of water.

## The Golden Ratio

The Specialty Coffee Association (SCA) recommends a **coffee-to-water ratio between 1:15 and 1:18 by weight** for filter methods. Baristas often shorthand this as:

- **1:15** — stronger, more body.
- **1:16** — the classic "golden ratio."
- **1:18** — lighter, cleaner.

Espresso is far more concentrated: typically **1:2** (e.g., 18 g coffee → 36 g liquid espresso in 25–30 seconds).

## Common brewing methods

### Espresso

- **Grind:** very fine.
- **Ratio:** 1:2 (e.g., 18 g in, 36 g out).
- **Water temperature:** 92–94 °C.
- **Time:** 25–32 seconds.
- **Pressure:** 9 bar.
- **Result:** 25–40 ml concentrated shot with crema.

### V60 (pour-over)

- **Grind:** medium-fine.
- **Ratio:** 1:16 (e.g., 15 g coffee, 240 g water).
- **Water temperature:** 92–96 °C.
- **Time:** 2:30–3:30 total.
- **Method:** 30 g bloom for 30 s, then pour in slow circles.
- **Result:** clean, bright, tea-like body.

### Chemex

- **Grind:** medium-coarse.
- **Ratio:** 1:16 to 1:17.
- **Time:** 4:00–5:00.
- **Paper filter:** 20–30% thicker than V60 → removes more oils → very clean cup.

### French press

- **Grind:** coarse.
- **Ratio:** 1:15.
- **Water temperature:** 93 °C.
- **Time:** 4:00, then plunge slowly.
- **Result:** heavy body, full mouthfeel, sediment.

### AeroPress

- **Grind:** medium-fine.
- **Ratio:** varies widely — 1:12 to 1:15 is common.
- **Time:** 1:00–2:30 depending on recipe.
- **Result:** clean, versatile, forgiving. Great for travel.

### Moka pot (stovetop espresso)

- **Grind:** fine-medium.
- **Method:** water boils in the base and is forced through the coffee bed by pressure (~1.5 bar).
- **Result:** strong, concentrated, slightly bitter. Not real espresso — no crema at 9 bar.

### Cold brew

- **Grind:** coarse.
- **Ratio:** 1:8 (concentrate) to 1:15 (ready-to-drink).
- **Time:** 12–24 hours at room temperature or in the fridge.
- **Result:** smooth, low acidity, high caffeine when made as concentrate.

## Water quality

Coffee is 98–99% water — so water matters. The SCA recommends:

- **Total dissolved solids (TDS):** 75–250 mg/L (ideal ~150 mg/L).
- **pH:** 6.5–7.5.
- **Hardness:** moderate. Very soft water under-extracts; very hard water tastes flat and scales equipment.

Use **fresh, filtered water** — never distilled (too soft) and never stale water from a kettle sitting overnight.

## Common defects and their causes

| Cup defect | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Sour, thin | Under-extraction: grind too coarse, water too cool, brew too short |
| Bitter, harsh | Over-extraction: grind too fine, water too hot, brew too long |
| Weak, watery | Ratio too low (not enough coffee), stale beans |
| Astringent, drying | Over-extraction, low-quality beans, poor water |
| Papery taste | Unrinsed paper filter (rinse Chemex/V60 filters before use) |
| Flat, no aroma | Stale coffee, wrong storage, poor water |
