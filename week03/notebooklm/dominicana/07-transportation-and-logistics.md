# Dominican Republic — Transportation and Logistics

Understanding transport in the DR is often the difference between a smooth trip and a stressful one. The country is bigger than it looks on a map (roughly the size of Ireland or Portugal) and driving times between regions are longer than most first-time visitors expect.

---

## International airports and typical use

| Airport | IATA | City | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Punta Cana Int'l | PUJ | Punta Cana | Eastern resorts (Bávaro, Uvero Alto, Cap Cana) |
| Las Américas Int'l | SDQ | Santo Domingo | Capital, south coast, Bayahibe |
| Gregorio Luperón | POP | Puerto Plata | Puerto Plata, Sosúa, Cabarete |
| El Catey Int'l | AZS | Samaná | Samaná Peninsula, Las Terrenas |
| Cibao Int'l | STI | Santiago | Cibao Valley, Jarabacoa |
| La Romana Int'l | LRM | La Romana | Casa de Campo, Bayahibe |

**Punta Cana (PUJ)** is unique in the world: it is a fully **privately owned airport** with a thatched-roof terminal. It handles more passengers than any other Caribbean airport.

### Airport arrival tips
- Complete the **Migración e-Ticket** online (free) within 72 hours of your flight — required for both arrival and departure. Print or screenshot the QR code.
- The **US$10 tourist card** is included in most airline tickets.
- On arrival: passport control, then baggage, then customs. Total time 20–90 min depending on incoming flights.
- **Departure taxes** are included in ticket price for scheduled flights.
- Foreign SIM cards work; local SIMs (Claro, Altice) are cheap (~US$10 for a tourist plan).

## Getting from the airport to your hotel

### Resort transfers
If you booked with a tour operator (TUI, Sunwing, Air Transat, Condor, Apple Vacations), the transfer is usually included. Look for your operator's rep at "Meeting Point A" outside customs.

### Private transfers (recommended for independent travelers)
Book in advance through your hotel, or through **Amstar, Nexus, Prestige** or a local operator. Fixed rates:

| Route | Approximate price (US$) |
|---|---|
| PUJ → Bávaro resorts | 30–50 |
| PUJ → Uvero Alto / Playa Blanca | 50–70 |
| PUJ → Cap Cana | 30–50 |
| SDQ → Zona Colonial | 40–60 |
| SDQ → Bayahibe | 90–130 |
| POP → Playa Dorada | 25–40 |
| POP → Cabarete | 35–55 |
| AZS → Las Terrenas | 40–60 |
| STI → Jarabacoa | 60–90 |

### Taxi at the airport
Available but usually 20–40% more expensive than pre-booked transfers. Confirm fare **before** getting in.

### Public transport from airports
Not practical from PUJ, POP, AZS. From **SDQ**, public buses run to central Santo Domingo (very cheap, but confusing for first-time visitors).

### Domestic flights
The DR has no scheduled domestic passenger network aside from Air Century and charter flights. Between regions, drive or fly to a different international airport.

## Long-distance buses

The DR has a first-class bus network — clean, air-conditioned, cheaper than most flights.

**Main operators:**
- **Caribe Tours** — the largest network, from Santo Domingo to almost every major town.
- **Metro Servicios Turísticos** — competitor with a similar network.
- **Expreso Bávaro** — Santo Domingo ↔ Punta Cana route (five daily departures each way, US$8 one-way).

**Example fares (one-way):**
- Santo Domingo ↔ Puerto Plata: 550 DOP (~US$10), 4 h.
- Santo Domingo ↔ Samaná: 470 DOP (~US$8), 3 h.
- Santo Domingo ↔ Punta Cana: 470 DOP (~US$8), 4 h.

Buses depart from Plaza Los Girasoles in Santo Domingo (Caribe Tours) and Av. Winston Churchill (Metro).

## Local transport

### Guagua (public minibus)
The everyday transport for Dominicans. Old vans and small buses running fixed routes. Cheap (20–100 DOP), crowded, and reliable in daylight. Adventurous but do-able for tourists on shorter regional hops.

### Motoconcho (motorcycle taxi)
Motorcycle taxi. Fastest way through beach-town traffic. **Wear a helmet** (often not provided; you can buy one for US$15 at any motoconcho stand). Fares: 50–300 DOP for in-town rides.

### Taxis
- **Airport taxis:** Fixed fares (see above).
- **Town taxis:** No meters. Ask price up-front. Typical: US$5–15 for short in-town trips; US$20–40 for cross-town.
- **Beach-resort taxis:** Notoriously expensive. A 20-min ride can be US$40. Sharing is common.

### Ride-hail apps
- **Uber** — works well in Santo Domingo, Santiago, and improving in Punta Cana.
- **inDrive** — works in most cities; you negotiate the price with the driver.
- **Apolo Taxi** — Santo Domingo-based; app-based dispatch.

Prices are 30–60% cheaper than street taxis. Recommended for solo travelers and after-dark rides.

### Public buses in Santo Domingo
Santo Domingo has a modern **metro** (two lines) and a **Teleférico** (cable car) system connecting the city with the north-side barrios. Cheap and safe. The metro is not close to tourist sites in Zona Colonial though.

## Car rental

### Should you rent?
- **Rent** if you plan to: base in Cabarete, do a Samaná loop, explore Barahona, mountain-hop Jarabacoa/Constanza, or stay a week in Las Terrenas and want independence.
- **Don't rent** if you're doing an all-inclusive in Punta Cana — you'll never use it.

### Rental basics
- All major agencies (Hertz, Avis, Europcar, Budget, Alamo) plus local operators.
- Small cars from ~US$40/day; SUV from US$60; 4x4 from US$85.
- **Insurance:** Local law requires third-party liability; most rentals include a basic package. Consider adding **CDW/theft** for peace of mind (about US$15/day extra).
- **Driver's license:** Your home country's license is valid for 90 days.
- **Age minimum:** 25 (most agencies); 21 with a young-driver surcharge.
- **Gas:** ~85 DOP/liter (~US$5.50/gallon). Full-to-full policy.

### Driving conditions
- **Highways** (Autopista del Este PUJ→SDQ, Autopista Duarte SDQ→POP): Modern toll roads. Tolls total ~US$5–10 one-way.
- **Secondary roads:** Variable. Potholes, speed bumps ("polícias acostados") often unsigned, livestock on the road.
- **Driving style:** Aggressive. Passing on the right, running amber lights, motorcycles weaving. Defensive driving essential.
- **Night driving:** Discouraged outside cities. Poor lighting, unexpected obstacles.
- **Rain:** Sudden tropical downpours; roads flood quickly.
- **GPS:** Use **Waze** (best for real-time traffic and detours) or **Google Maps**.

### Police checkpoints
Common but generally professional. Slow down, be polite, show driver's license and passport (or copy). Bribery is not expected of tourists — a real ticket, though, is possible if you're speeding or unbuckled.

## Special-case logistics

### Ferry Samaná ↔ Sabana de la Mar
Passenger ferry across Samaná Bay. Saves 3 hours of driving. Runs 3× daily. Small vessel; can be rough.

### Boat transfer Bayahibe ↔ Saona Island
Only reachable by boat (catamaran or speedboat). All excursion operators run daily trips.

### Getting to Bahía de las Águilas
The last stretch is only reachable by boat from Cabo Rojo. Boats leave when full (or hire private for ~US$60).

## Connectivity and money on the move

- **Local SIM:** Buy a **Claro** or **Altice** tourist SIM at the airport for US$10–20 for 5–10 GB and unlimited local calls. Passport needed to register.
- **eSIM:** Airalo, Holafly, Nomad all cover DR. Buy before travel.
- **ATMs:** Every town has ATMs. Withdraw pesos — better rates than dollar exchange. Common ATM limit: 10,000 DOP (~US$170) per transaction. Fees ~US$5.
- **Currency exchange:** Better rate at banks than at hotels; worst at airports.
- **Credit cards:** Accepted at hotels, chain restaurants, big supermarkets. Expect a **5% "credit card fee"** in some tourist shops. **Cash is king** in local restaurants, taxis, tips, and markets.

## Common logistical mistakes to avoid

1. **Booking Punta Cana thinking day trips to Santo Domingo are easy.** They exist, but 5 hours of driving is a heavy day.
2. **Renting a car for a Punta Cana all-inclusive.** You'll leave it in the parking lot.
3. **Under-tipping transfer drivers.** US$5–10 tip is standard for a 30–60 min transfer with luggage.
4. **Skipping the e-Ticket.** You cannot board without the QR code (in principle) — do it early.
5. **Trusting Google Maps timings blindly.** Add 30–50% for traffic in cities and rural detours in the south and southwest.
6. **Assuming there are quick highway shortcuts.** The northeast to southeast route (Samaná → Punta Cana) is scenic but slow — 4+ hours.
