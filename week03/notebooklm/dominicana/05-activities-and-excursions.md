# Dominican Republic — Activities and Excursions

This document is a practical menu of things to do beyond lying on the beach. It includes the most popular guided excursions, watersports, adventure activities, cultural experiences, and honest notes on which are worth the price.

---

## Signature guided excursions (the "must-do" list)

### Saona Island (Bayahibe)
The country's most popular full-day excursion. Combines a catamaran cruise, a stop at a natural pool where you can stand chest-deep in the ocean surrounded by starfish, and 2–3 hours on Saona's palm-lined beach with an included lunch.
- **Duration:** 9 hours (7:30 pick-up).
- **Price:** US$85–140 depending on operator and pick-up location.
- **Verdict:** Genuinely spectacular. Book with reputable operators (Bávaro Splash, Seavis) to avoid overcrowded party boats.

### Whale watching in Samaná Bay
Mid-January to mid-March. Half-day boat trip from Samaná town. Expect multiple humpback sightings within an hour of leaving the harbor. Recommended operator: **Kim Beddall / Whale Samana** (the pioneer of DR whale watching since 1985).
- **Duration:** 3.5 hours.
- **Price:** US$65 adults, US$35 children.
- **Verdict:** A bucket-list experience. Book with an operator that uses hydrophones so you can hear the whales sing.

### 27 Charcos de Damajagua (Puerto Plata)
27 natural waterfalls in a canyon. Jump and slide down each in turn. Life vests and helmets included. Adventure-holiday classic.
- **Duration:** Half day; on the water about 2 hours.
- **Price:** US$45–65 with transport.
- **Verdict:** Wonderful for adults and older kids (10+). Skip if you have back or knee problems.

### El Limón Waterfall (Samaná)
A 40 m waterfall reached by horseback (40 min each way) or on foot (weather-dependent). Includes swim time in the deep pool below.
- **Duration:** Half day.
- **Price:** US$30–50 including horses and lunch.
- **Verdict:** Beautiful. Horse conditions vary by ranch — choose one that emphasizes animal welfare (Rancho Cascada, Santi Rancho).

### Los Haitises National Park (from Samaná or Sabana de la Mar)
Boat trip through mangroves and sea caves; visit Taíno pictographs; bird-watch (frigate birds, pelicans, brown boobies).
- **Duration:** Half day.
- **Price:** US$60–90.
- **Verdict:** Highly recommended for nature lovers.

### Santo Domingo Zona Colonial day trip
From most resorts, a long day (10–12 hours). Cathedral, Alcázar de Colón, Fortaleza Ozama, Calle Las Damas. Combine with Los Tres Ojos underground lakes.
- **Duration:** Full day.
- **Price:** US$85–140.
- **Verdict:** Worth doing once — but the drive from Punta Cana is 2.5 hours each way. If your itinerary allows, spend a night in Zona Colonial instead.

### Isla Catalina snorkeling and diving
From Bayahibe. Shallow reef teeming with fish; the "Catalina Wall" for divers. Buffet on the beach.
- **Duration:** Full day.
- **Price:** US$75–120.

### Cayo Arena / Paradise Island (Punta Rucia, north coast)
A **tiny** sand bar in shallow water 15 min offshore. Excellent snorkeling and photo backdrops. Often combined with a mangrove tour.
- **Duration:** Full day.
- **Price:** US$85–120.

## Watersports

### Kitesurfing and windsurfing (Cabarete)
Cabarete's **Kite Beach** is a world-class kite destination with predictable side-onshore winds 18–25 knots, June to August especially. Schools include Kite Beach House, Laurel Eastman Kiteboarding, and Kite Club Cabarete.
- **Beginner package (6-hour course):** US$300–400.
- **Advanced rentals:** US$80–120/day.

### Surfing (Playa Encuentro, Cabarete)
The best surf beach in the country. Consistent right and left breaks. Playa Macao (east) and Playa Preciosa (north) are secondary options.

### Diving certifications
PADI Open Water courses run at every major resort area:
- Bayahibe: **~US$430** for 3-day certification.
- Sosúa: **~US$400**.
- Punta Cana: **~US$500** (more expensive due to resort pricing).

### Deep-sea fishing (Punta Cana, Boca de Yuma, Cabeza de Toro)
Blue marlin, sailfish, mahi-mahi, wahoo. Charters US$800–1,600 for a half-day.

### Sailing catamaran cruises
Half-day tours from every major beach town. Snorkeling stop + open bar + lunch. US$70–120.

### Snuba and Sea-Bob
Popular resort activities — no certification needed. US$60–100 per session.

## Adventure and outdoor

### Hiking Pico Duarte (3,098 m)
The Caribbean's highest peak. 46 km round-trip over 2–3 days from La Ciénaga (Jarabacoa) or Sabaneta. Mandatory guide + mules. Basic huts along the trail.
- **Cost:** US$250–400 per person all-in.
- **Best months:** December–March (dry, cool, clear summit views).

### Canyoning (Jarabacoa)
Salto de Baiguate and other canyons. Rappelling, jumping, natural water slides.
- **Cost:** US$80–120.

### Whitewater rafting (Río Yaque del Norte, Jarabacoa)
Class II–III on the Caribbean's longest river.
- **Cost:** US$60–95.

### Zip-line canopy tours
Multiple in Punta Cana (Bávaro Runners, Scape Park), Puerto Plata, and Samaná.
- **Cost:** US$70–120.

### Buggy and ATV tours
Popular half-day tours through countryside, sugar-cane fields, and a local farm/coffee stop.
- **Cost:** US$60–120.
- **Verdict:** Fun but dusty. Wear old clothes and sunglasses.

### Horseback riding
Options in every region. Best on empty beaches at sunset (Uvero Alto, Cabarete, Playa Grande).
- **Cost:** US$40–80 for 1–2 hours.

### Paragliding (Jarabacoa)
Tandem flights over the Cordillera Central.
- **Cost:** US$110 for a 20-min flight.

## Cultural and city experiences

### Zona Colonial walking tour (Santo Domingo)
Free guided walking tours leave daily from Parque Colón at 10:00. Two hours. Tip the guide US$10–20.

### Faro a Colón (Columbus Lighthouse)
Massive cruciform monument on the outskirts of Santo Domingo. Houses a mausoleum said to hold Christopher Columbus's remains (disputed).

### Museo del Ámbar (Puerto Plata) and Museo Mundo de Ámbar (Santo Domingo)
Dominican amber is world-renowned. The **Puerto Plata Amber Museum** displays the "mosquito in amber" that inspired *Jurassic Park*. Free to enter; buy jewelry downstairs.

### Larimar mine visit (Barahona)
Larimar is a pale-blue pectolite stone found only in this country. Visit the working mines near Filipinas, then buy directly from artisans.

### Altos de Chavón (La Romana)
A recreated 16th-century Mediterranean village on a hilltop overlooking the Chavón River. Amphitheatre, artist studios, church, restaurants. The most photographed inland site in the country.

### Cigar factory tours (Santiago and La Romana)
La Aurora (Santiago) and Tabacalera de García (La Romana) offer tours of the country's premium cigar production. Free samples.

### Rum tours (Barahona and San Pedro)
**Brugal**, **Barceló**, and **Bermúdez** are the "3 Bs" of Dominican rum. Factory tours in Puerto Plata (Brugal) and San Pedro de Macorís (Bermúdez).

### Baseball game
The national obsession. **Liga Dominicana de Béisbol Profesional (LIDOM)** plays October–January. Watching a game in San Pedro de Macorís, La Romana, or Santo Domingo is a cultural highlight even for non-fans.
- **Tickets:** US$5–30.

### Merengue and bachata dance classes
Available at boutique hotels, cultural centers, and by private tutor. US$15–40 per hour.

## Family-friendly favorites

- **Dolphin Island Park (Bávaro):** swim with dolphins, sharks (nurse), stingrays. Ethically debated; some operators are better than others.
- **Manatí Park (Bávaro):** dolphin shows, cultural performances, small zoo.
- **Ocean World (Puerto Plata):** dolphin and sea lion shows.
- **Scape Park (Cap Cana):** zip lines, Hoyo Azul cenote, cave swimming.
- **Marinarium (Bávaro):** shallow-water snorkeling with rays and sharks in a huge natural aquarium.
- **Bávaro Splash (Bávaro):** kid-friendly speedboat and snorkel combo.

## Booking tips

- **Book direct with reputable operators** (Bávaro Splash, Seavis, Whale Samana, Iguana Mama, Rancho Baiguate). Resort concierges take 20–40% commission and may push mediocre tours.
- **Cash tips:** Guides, drivers, and boat crews earn most of their income from tips. US$5–10 per guest per activity is generous.
- **Health considerations:** For active excursions with jumps or hikes, be honest about fitness and joint issues. Rescue from remote canyons is difficult.
- **Insurance:** Travel insurance with adventure-activity coverage is strongly recommended if you plan to kite, dive, or hike Pico Duarte.
