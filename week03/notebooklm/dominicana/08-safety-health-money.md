# Dominican Republic — Safety, Health, and Money

Realistic, non-alarmist information for planning a Dominican Republic vacation. The DR is one of the safest and most tourist-ready countries in the Caribbean, but it is a developing country with its own quirks.

---

## Safety overview

### Where you'll actually go
- **Tourist zones** (Bávaro, Uvero Alto, Cap Cana, Bayahibe, Puerto Plata resort strip, Cabarete, Las Terrenas, Zona Colonial in Santo Domingo): heavily policed by the **CESTUR** tourist police (green uniforms). Very safe by day and night for common tourist behavior.
- **Working neighborhoods and unofficial beaches**: petty theft is the main issue. Use hotel safes; don't leave bags on the beach.

### Petty crime
The most common tourist incidents:
- Snatch-and-run bag theft from motorbikes (rare, but happens in cities).
- Hotel-room theft (rare in reputable properties; always use the safe).
- Beach theft while swimming (leave valuables at the resort).
- Overcharging at unofficial taxis and street vendors — negotiate first.

### Serious crime
Violent crime against tourists is uncommon. Most incidents involve:
- Late-night solo walking outside tourist zones.
- Encounters with sex workers or drugs, both of which invite scams and worse.
- Unauthorized excursions with unlicensed guides.

### Scams to watch for
- **Fake "guides"** at tourist sites (Zona Colonial, Puerto Plata) offering to walk you around — pay a police-approved guide instead.
- **Rigged taxi meters** or "extra fees" — pre-negotiate.
- **Timeshare pitches** disguised as free tours, breakfasts, or scratch cards.
- **"Broken" jet skis** rented on the beach — inspect and photograph before paying.
- **"Emergency assistance"** on the highway — helpful strangers who then demand payment.
- **Credit card skimming** — use ATMs inside banks or resorts.

### Practical safety habits
- Carry a **photocopy** of your passport; keep the original in the safe.
- **Don't flash** valuables (large cameras, jewelry, cash).
- **Ride-hail apps** are safer than street taxis after dark.
- Learn basic Spanish greetings — courtesy defuses most encounters.
- Register with your embassy's traveler alert system if available.
- In cities, use only **ATMs inside banks or hotels**.

### Emergency numbers
| Service | Number |
|---|---|
| National emergency (all) | **911** |
| Tourist police (CESTUR) | 1-809-200-3500 |
| Consumer protection (Pro Consumidor) | 1-809-200-2432 |
| Fire | 911 (or 809-682-2000) |

**911 works nationally** and dispatchers speak English (usually).

## Health

### Vaccinations
No compulsory vaccinations for entry from most countries. Recommended:
- **Routine vaccines** up to date (MMR, DPT, hepatitis A/B, flu).
- **Hepatitis A and Typhoid** — for travelers eating widely off-resort.
- **Yellow fever certificate** required only if arriving from a yellow-fever country.

### Mosquito-borne diseases
- **Dengue** — present year-round; outbreaks in rainy season.
- **Chikungunya** — sporadic.
- **Zika** — pregnant women should consult a doctor before travel.
- **Malaria** — very low risk, only in specific rural border areas; not the tourist zones.

**Best defense:** DEET or picaridin repellent, long sleeves at dusk, air-conditioned rooms. Resorts fumigate regularly.

### Water and food safety
- **Don't drink tap water.** Bottled water is universal and cheap.
- **Ice at resorts and reputable restaurants** is fine (made from filtered water).
- **Fresh salads and unpeeled fruits** — safe at resorts and mid-range restaurants; skip at roadside stalls.
- **Seafood** — very safe at coastal restaurants; use judgment inland.
- **"Traveler's stomach"** is the most common tourist ailment — pack Imodium and oral rehydration salts.

### Sun and heat
- **UV index** commonly 10–12 (extreme). Sunburn happens in 20 minutes at midday.
- Use SPF 50+ waterproof sunscreen. Reef-safe formulas required at some sites.
- Drink 3–4 liters of water per day; even more if drinking alcohol.
- Beware of dehydration masking as hangover.

### Medical care
- Private hospitals in Santo Domingo (Hospital Metropolitano, HOMS Santiago, Centro Médico Punta Cana) are modern and English-speaking.
- Public hospitals are basic — private is strongly preferred.
- Travel insurance with **medical evacuation** coverage is essential. Standard travel insurance covers most needs; for adventure activities (kite, dive, hike), get an adventure-sports rider.
- Common resort clinic visit: US$50–100. Hospital ER admission: US$500+.

### Pharmacies
- Every town has multiple pharmacies. **Farmacias Carol** and **Farmax** are the two big chains.
- Many drugs sold over-the-counter (some antibiotics, muscle relaxants).
- Bring prescription meds in original containers with a doctor's note if regulated.

### Common ailments and prevention
- **Sunburn**: aloe vera, hats, umbrella by the pool.
- **Stomach upset**: probiotic beforehand; Imodium onboard.
- **Cuts on coral**: rinse with fresh water, apply antibiotic ointment (coral cuts infect quickly in tropical water).
- **Jellyfish/Portuguese man o' war**: occasionally on east coast. Vinegar for the sting; do NOT use fresh water.
- **Sea urchins**: on rocky snorkel spots. Wear water shoes.
- **Sand fleas** ("no-see-ums"): worst at dusk near mangroves. Use repellent.

## Money

### Currency
- **Dominican Peso (DOP)**, symbol RD$.
- Approximate rate: 1 USD ≈ 58 DOP (fluctuates; check current rate at [xe.com](https://www.xe.com) before travel).
- Coins: 1, 5, 10, 25 pesos. Bills: 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000, 2000 pesos.

### US Dollar
- Widely accepted at hotels, big restaurants, taxis, and tour operators — but usually at a worse conversion rate than paying pesos.
- **Small bills** (US$1, 5, 10, 20) are more useful than large ones.
- **Torn or old US bills** are often refused. Bring crisp, post-2013 notes.

### ATMs
- Universal in cities and tourist areas. Withdraw pesos.
- Withdrawal limit: usually 10,000 DOP (~US$170) per transaction. Some ATMs allow up to 20,000 DOP.
- Fees: local bank ~200 DOP + your home bank's international fee.
- Best ATMs: **BanReservas** (state bank), **Popular**, **Scotiabank**.

### Credit cards
- Accepted at all resorts, chain restaurants, supermarkets, and gas stations.
- **Visa and Mastercard** widely accepted; **AmEx** less so.
- Some small shops add a 5% "surcharge" for credit-card use.
- **Chip and PIN** — check that your card supports PIN.
- Notify your bank of travel dates.
- **Skimming** is a risk at unattended ATMs; prefer ATMs inside banks.

### Traveler's checks
Effectively dead. Don't bother.

### Tipping — the full guide

Restaurants automatically add **10% service charge** (propina legal) + **18% ITBIS tax**. On top of the propina legal:
- **Good service:** additional 5–10% cash tip.
- **Excellent service:** 15% total.

Other tipping conventions:
- **Housekeeping:** US$1–3 per day, cash left on the pillow daily.
- **Bellman/porter:** US$1–2 per bag.
- **Tour guides:** US$5–10 per person per day.
- **Bus drivers on tours:** US$3–5 per person.
- **Airport-transfer drivers:** US$5–10 (more for late-night or bad weather).
- **Taxi drivers:** Round up; not required.
- **Beach chair vendors, jet ski operators:** Not customary but appreciated.
- **Salon/spa:** 10–15%.

**Currency of tipping:** US dollars or pesos both fine. US$1 or 50–100 DOP notes are the most useful tip denominations.

### Budgeting

Sample daily costs (per person, mid-range independent traveler):
| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Guesthouse or 3-star hotel | US$60–120 |
| Meals (breakfast, lunch, dinner) | US$25–45 |
| Local transport | US$5–15 |
| Excursions (average) | US$40–80 |
| Beers/drinks (2–3) | US$5–15 |
| **Total** | **US$130–275/day** |

All-inclusive resort travelers effectively spend US$150–450/day once you factor in flight, resort, and off-resort activities.

### Taxes
- **ITBIS (VAT):** 18% on most goods and restaurant meals.
- **Hotel tax:** 18% + 10% service, usually included in all-inclusive rates.
- **Departure tax:** Included in most airline tickets (US$20).

## Insurance

Strongly recommended, especially for:
- Adventure activities (diving, kitesurfing, canyoning, Pico Duarte).
- Anyone over 60.
- Anyone with pre-existing conditions.
- Trips longer than a week.

Look for a policy with:
- **Medical evacuation** coverage of at least US$100,000.
- **Trip cancellation** for hurricane/named-storm scenarios (buy before storm is named).
- **Baggage loss.**
- **Adventure sports rider** if applicable.

Popular options: Allianz, World Nomads, IMG, SafetyWing.

---

## Hurricanes and weather disruptions

- **Season:** June 1 – November 30. Peak: mid-August to early October.
- The DR is often spared major hits (the mountains disrupt storm structure).
- Resorts have well-rehearsed hurricane protocols; airlines re-book ahead of storms.
- If a hurricane is named and headed toward the DR while you're there, follow resort staff instructions — do not attempt to leave independently.

## Language

Spanish is the official language. In tourist zones, English is widely spoken by staff. Learn basic greetings — the country's warmth to visitors goes up dramatically when you try:
- **Hola / Buenos días / Buenas tardes / Buenas noches** — hello / good morning / afternoon / evening.
- **Por favor / Gracias / De nada** — please / thank you / you're welcome.
- **Sí / No / Tal vez** — yes / no / maybe.
- **La cuenta, por favor** — the bill, please.
- **¿Cuánto cuesta?** — how much?
- **Perdón / Disculpe** — sorry / excuse me.
- **No hablo mucho español** — I don't speak much Spanish.
- **¿Habla inglés?** — do you speak English?
